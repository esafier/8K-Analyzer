Read this before trusting the numbers above. A coin flip scores ~50%, so a hit rate near 50% means the signal is not adding anything. Rates are withheld entirely below {{ card.min_sample }} scored calls rather than shown with a caveat. Horizons are calendar days from the filing date, and each window is compared against SPY over exactly the same dates. These are not tradeable returns. The baseline is the closing price on the filing date, and an 8-K that drops after the 4pm close is not public at that price — so for those filings the measured move includes the overnight reaction, which nobody could have traded on. That inflates apparent performance, and 5.02 departure filings land after hours often. Treat these numbers as a read on whether the signal detects something real, not as a backtest. Nothing here is survivorship-adjusted either, and that bias runs against the bearish signal: a name whose symbol stopped resolving is scored at every horizon it actually traded through, but its later horizons are dropped — and those dropped outcomes are disproportionately the ones where the bearish case was coming true. Read a bearish hit rate here as a floor, not a ceiling.
